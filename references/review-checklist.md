# Professional writing review checklist

Run the review in order. Fix argument problems before sentence polish.

## Purpose and audience

- Can the central claim be stated in one sentence?
- Is the desired reader understanding, judgment, or action clear?
- Does every major section advance that outcome?

## Argument

- Does the need lead naturally to the challenge, gap, and solution?
- Are abstract difficulties converted into concrete causes or conflicts?
- Is the solution introduced only after its design requirements are clear?
- Does each section have one principal argumentative function?
- Does each substantive paragraph make a claim and explain its significance?

## Evidence

- Is every major claim supported by an appropriate type of evidence?
- Does the claim stay within the evidence's population, conditions, and protocol?
- Are causal, novelty, and superiority claims justified?
- Are missing data or references separated from polished prose rather than disguised?
- Are examples identified as illustrations rather than general proof?

## Concepts and terminology

- Are background, conditions, constraints, objectives, methods, metrics, and evidence distinguished?
- Are unfamiliar terms defined at first use?
- Is one term used consistently for each concept?
- Are symbols, units, acronyms, and names consistent across text and visuals?

## Style

- Remove work-log phrases, revision commentary, code-level narration, and status reporting unless required by genre.
- Replace defensive disclaimers with direct, evidence-bounded claims.
- Replace empty praise with mechanism or evidence.
- Remove repeated background, conditions, parameters, and numerical recitations.
- Check that transitions explain why the next topic follows.

## Quantitative presentation

- Do the statistics match the distribution and task objective?
- Are denominators, filters, thresholds, and comparison protocols consistent?
- Do figures, tables, and prose have distinct roles?
- Does the text interpret the key pattern instead of narrating every mark or value?

## References and integrity

- Does each citation directly support its neighboring claim?
- Are original or primary sources used when appropriate?
- Are bibliographic details, quotations, data, and equations verified?
- Are placeholders, unresolved references, and unsupported assertions removed?

## Final artifact

- Are figures and tables near their first discussion?
- Are fonts, labels, legends, and line styles readable at final size?
- Are there avoidable blank areas, isolated small figures, clipping, or overflow?
- Are headings, numbering, cross-references, and captions correct?
- Has the final rendered document—not only the source—been reviewed page by page?
